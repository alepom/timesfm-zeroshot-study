\section{Conclusion}

In the domains and periods evaluated, TimesFM~3 forecasts post-release data more accurately than the pre-specified classical
ensemble, and the point-forecast advantage survives other aggregations, stricter inference, a shorter context and a re-specified
baseline for river flow. Its lead over Chronos-2 is small, sensitive to inferential choices and, on hourly data, grows with the
length of the context, from parity at 2{,}016 points; over TimesFM~2.5 it is resolved only for the quantile loss. On a 30-task sample
the published benchmark results largely reproduce, although the published comparison with Chronos-2 mixes inference options. The
Wikipedia comparison does not identify an effect of memorisation. On exchange rates, yields and oil prices every foundation model
we ran was worse than the random walk, and saturating growth and recent level shifts in synthetic data show failures worth guarding
against with simple baselines. Zero-shot forecasting has gone far, but not everywhere.
