\section{Conclusion}

In the domains and periods evaluated, TimesFM~3 forecasts post-release data more accurately than the pre-specified classical
ensemble, and the point-forecast advantage holds under other aggregations, the small-cluster and non-overlapping-window checks, a
shorter context and a re-specified baseline for river flow; the size of the probabilistic advantage depends on the weighting and on
the river baseline. Its lead over Chronos-2 is small and sensitive to inferential choices; on hourly data it is not resolved when both
models see 2{,}016 points, and whether TimesFM~3 gains more than Chronos-2 from a longer context is not resolved. Over TimesFM~2.5
the lead is resolved only for the quantile loss. On a 30-task sample
the published benchmark results largely reproduce, although the published comparison with Chronos-2 mixes inference options. The
Wikipedia comparison does not identify an effect of memorisation. On exchange rates, yields and oil prices every foundation model
we ran was worse than the random walk, and, in synthetic data, a saturating process and recent upward level shifts produce failures
worth guarding against with simple baselines. Zero-shot forecasting has gone far, but not everywhere.
