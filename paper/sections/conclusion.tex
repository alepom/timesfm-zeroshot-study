\section{Conclusion}

TimesFM~3 delivers on data it cannot have seen: it is clearly more accurate than a strong classical ensemble on post-release
electricity, weather and river data, and slightly more accurate than Chronos-2 and its predecessor. Its benchmark lead over other
foundation models is narrower than the published comparison suggests, because the comparison mixes inference options. We find no
trace of memorisation where exposure is documented. The model should not be used on near-random-walk series, and its forecasts
should be checked against simple baselines after regime changes and on saturating growth. Zero-shot forecasting has gone far, but
not everywhere.
